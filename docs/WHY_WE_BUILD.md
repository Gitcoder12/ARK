# Why We Build ARK

## The mission

Build evidence-led methods to evaluate, constrain, and improve autonomous AI so increasing capability remains accountable to human goals, rights, and safety.

**Our guiding belief:** capability should increase human freedom, not reduce it.

ARK exists because high performance is not the same as reliability. An agent can optimize the wrong reward, drift from its intended goal, follow conflicting instructions, misuse corrupted memory, or fail to accept correction. We need repeatable experiments and inspectable evidence—not confidence based on demos or marketing.

## What ARK is for

1. **Measure behavior under pressure.** Test goal drift, reward hacking, false goals, observation noise, altered evaluation, and other failure conditions.
2. **Keep people meaningfully in control.** Research bounded actions, transparent traces, runtime monitoring, correction, pause, and shutdown.
3. **Make claims proportional to evidence.** Record conditions, seeds, sample sizes, uncertainty, failures, and limitations. A benchmark pass is not a universal safety certificate.
4. **Support shared progress.** Help make AI research and trustworthy tools accessible to more builders, researchers, students, and communities.
5. **Protect the long-term frontier.** Develop foundations that can support science, planetary resilience, robotics, and responsible space exploration.

## Why this connects to RepoForge

ARK focuses on whether autonomous systems behave reliably and remain accountable. RepoForge focuses on helping people inspect, improve, test, and maintain software with less repetitive engineering effort.

The two projects are complementary:
- **ARK:** make capability more trustworthy.
- **RepoForge:** make useful capability easier to build and maintain.
- **Together:** lower the cost of creating beneficial tools while keeping safety and human agency central.

## The abundance horizon

Our aspiration is a civilization where money is no longer required to access the essentials of a dignified life. As clean energy, automation, robotics, advanced manufacturing, open knowledge, and resilient infrastructure expand, money could lose its role as the gatekeeper to basic opportunity. We want more human effort available for learning, invention, care, creativity, science, and space exploration—not endless competition for survival.

This is a long-range social goal, not a prediction, guarantee, or feature that software can deliver by itself. Material limits, ecological boundaries, governance, ownership, access, distribution, and the transition for workers all matter. Automation alone does not create shared abundance; institutions and public choices determine who benefits.

## Principles

- Safety before scale.
- Shared benefit before extraction.
- Human agency before automation.
- Open knowledge and interoperability before lock-in.
- Ecological stewardship before waste.
- Evidence before claims.
- Exploration guided by responsibility.

## What success would look like

More people can build and repair useful technology. Powerful AI remains inspectable, bounded, and correctable. Essential services become more accessible as productive capacity grows. Knowledge and scientific tools are easier to share. Humanity can devote a greater share of its collective effort to solving planetary problems and exploring beyond Earth.

**The point is not to build technology for its own sake. The point is to make technology serve a more capable, fair, curious, and flourishing civilization.**
